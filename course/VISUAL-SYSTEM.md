# Course visual system

The guide uses the same visual approach as the companion evaluation courses:
Mermaid diagrams for relationships, emojis as navigation cues, and a small
palette whose colors reinforce labels without carrying meaning by themselves.

## Palette

| Role | Color | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cream | `#F7F1E8` | context, page, or neutral starting point |
| Navy | `#16324F` | headings, labels, and primary outline |
| Blue | `#DCECF3` / `#2F6B8A` | input, context, or observation |
| Orange | `#F5D8C4` / `#D97941` | evidence, evaluation, or decision |
| Sage | `#DCE8D8` / `#6F8F72` | verified continuation, recovery, or allowed action |
| Red | `#F3D6D2` / `#B44B45` | missing evidence, unsafe action, or escalation |

Red is reserved for a degraded or blocking path. Every diagram also uses
explicit labels and arrows, so a learner does not need to distinguish colors
to understand the lesson.

## Diagram conventions

- Keep one relationship per diagram.
- Keep diagrams to seven major nodes or fewer.
- Use blue for input and observation.
- Use orange for evidence and decisions.
- Use sage for verified continuation.
- Use red for stop, block, or escalation.
- Use a text label for every state that color also represents.

## Emoji legend

- 🧭 orientation or course route
- 🎯 audience, objective, or decision
- 🧪 learner practice or synthetic experiment
- 📝 assessment or evidence record
- 🗣️ teach-back or facilitator communication
- 🔄 revision or iteration
- 🛡️ safety, approval, privacy, or access boundary
- 📊 measurement or adoption evidence
- ⚠️ limitation or unestablished claim

The emojis are signposts, not assessment labels. A learner can complete the
course without relying on emoji or color.
