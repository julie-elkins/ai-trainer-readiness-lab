# Pilot protocol

A **pilot** is a small first delivery used to find course problems before
repeating the course. It is not a validation study, a credential trial, or
evidence that the course transfers to real work.

## Scope

Run the pilot with one to three learners using the same synthetic scenario,
worksheet, scorecard, teach-back, and learner feedback form described in the
course guide.

Do not use private learner records, production traces, confidential work
information, unpublished exam material, or provider credentials.

## Before the pilot

1. Record the course version and delivery date.
2. Confirm that the positive and degraded examples are available.
3. Prepare one blank [learner worksheet](../templates/learner-worksheet.md)
   per learner.
4. Prepare one [facilitator scorecard](../templates/facilitator-scorecard.md)
   per learner.
5. Prepare the [learner feedback form](../templates/learner-feedback.md).
6. State the stop condition: stop the pilot if the task requires private data,
   external action, or a product account.

## During the pilot

For each learner:

1. deliver the 60-minute workshop;
2. score the artifact using the scorecard;
3. observe the teach-back and record an evidence note;
4. offer the learner feedback form after the workshop; and
5. record accessibility or delivery issues without recording identifiers.

Keep the course timing and task contract the same across pilot participants.
If the facilitator changes an instruction during delivery, record that change
as a pilot observation rather than silently treating the deliveries as
identical.

## After the pilot

Complete the [pilot summary](../templates/pilot-summary.md) using themes, not
raw learner responses.

Compare three layers:

- **Performance evidence:** rubric scores, worksheet evidence, and teach-back.
- **Experience evidence:** clarity, friction, accessibility, and confidence
  changes reported in the feedback form.
- **Delivery evidence:** timing, facilitator prompts, and materials that were
  missing or confusing.

Use the comparison to choose one action:

- **Retain:** the course element worked and the evidence supports keeping it.
- **Clarify:** the objective was understood but the instruction was ambiguous.
- **Revise:** a specific material or activity needs a targeted change.
- **Retry:** the task or support was not usable enough to interpret the result.

Record the decision in the
[workshop revision record](../templates/workshop-revision-record.md).

## Interpretation boundary

Do not average feedback into a course score. Do not treat confidence change,
positive comments, completion, or attendance as proof of learning. Do not
generalize from one to three learners. The pilot identifies what to inspect
next; it does not establish reliability, transfer, product quality, or
credential validity.
